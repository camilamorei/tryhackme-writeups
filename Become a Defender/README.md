Task 2 - Understanding Your Environment

Before protecting an environment, defenders need visibility into what exists and how the different systems are connected.

Key Defensive Concepts
Prevention - Security controls designed to stop attacks before they happen.
Detection - Monitoring systems and networks to identify suspicious activity.
Mitigation - Actions taken during an incident to limit damage.
Analysis - Reviewing logs and evidence to understand what happened and what was affected.
Response and Improvement - Recovering from incidents and improving defenses.

Task 3 - Defending Your Environment

Defenders need to understand how attackers could move through an environment and apply protections at different stages.

Defender Principles
Threat anticipation - Consider realistic ways an attacker could compromise systems.
Attack awareness - Understand common attack stages and attack chains.
Risk prioritization - Identify high-value and critical systems so security efforts can be focused where they matter most.
Continuous adaptation - Continuously improve defenses as threats and vulnerabilities evolve.
Available Defenses
Employee Devices - Malicious links or unsafe software - Antivirus and software updates
Web Server - Attempts to compromise the website - Safe traffic and secure communication
Mail Server - Malicious or deceptive emails - Spam filters and attachment scanning
Firewall - Unauthorized internet access - Firewall rules and blocking known threats
Outside Internet - External threats - Restrict inbound traffic and monitor suspicious activity

Task 4 - Where to Go From Here

The room introduced important defensive security terminology and career paths.

Key Terminology
Blue Team - Cybersecurity defenders responsible for protecting systems and responding to threats.
Client Infrastructure - Networks, servers, devices, and applications that need protection.
Visibility - The ability to monitor activity across systems.
Threat - A potential danger that could harm systems or data.
Prevention - Stopping threats before they cause harm.
Detection - Identifying threats or suspicious activity.
Mitigation - Reducing or stopping the impact of a threat.
Risk - The likelihood and potential impact of a threat successfully causing harm.
Career Opportunities
SOC (Security Operations Center) - Monitors systems and investigates suspicious activity.
Threat Intelligence - Researches threats, attackers, and trends.
DFIR (Digital Forensics & Incident Response) - Investigates incidents, contains threats, and helps restore affected systems.
